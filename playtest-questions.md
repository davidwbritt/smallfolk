# Smallfolk — playtest question kit

For seeding to gamer friends. "What do you think?" gets you "cool!" —
these get you a roadmap. Send each friend a **specific seed link** rather
than the homepage ("meet Ostenmoor — the shepherd robbed two graves" gets
opened; a generic tool link gets bookmarked and forgotten).

Sample seed to send: https://davidwbritt.github.io/smallfolk/#bram-combe-7
Worked session, for the RPG-curious: https://davidwbritt.github.io/smallfolk/example.html

## The five questions

1. **What's the first thing you clicked — and the first thing you looked
   for that wasn't there?**
   The second half of that question is where features hide.

2. **Would you run this sheet at your table this week, as-is?
   If no — what's the ONE missing thing?**
   The only bar that matters; "one thing" forces prioritization instead
   of a wishlist.

3. **Which parts would you trust in play, and which would you rewrite?**
   Gets a per-module quality report (NPCs vs. rumors vs. Trouble vs. map)
   instead of a vague overall impression.

4. **What system do you run, and what would make this feel native to it?**
   Prediction: the most common answer will be *stat blocks* — worth
   knowing which system's format before deciding if that's in-scope.

5. **Did you feel like sending a village to anyone? Why not?**
   The virality test — and "why not" is the actual question.

## For solo players, add

6. **Did Player view + the oracle give you a mystery you could actually
   play without spoiling yourself?**

## Reading the answers

- Weigh what they **do** over what they **say**: a friend who sends a seed
  back is worth five who say it's neat.
- Repeated asks beat loud asks: track counts, not enthusiasm.
- Bring everything back raw — Claude sorts it into ship-now / ship-later /
  politely-never, and builds.
